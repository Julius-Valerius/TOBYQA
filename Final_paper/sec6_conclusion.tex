\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Standard model-based derivative-free methods construct local interpolation models from sequential function evaluations under the assumption that the objective is stationary. When observations are corrupted by both random noise and temporal drift, unmodeled drift biases the fitted gradient along the sampling trajectory and causes trust-region methods to reject descent steps and contract their radius prematurely. In this paper, we have proposed TOBYQA to address this setting using $2n+1$ active sample points. By augmenting a regularized minimum-Frobenius-norm quadratic model with a linear temporal term, TOBYQA decouples the spatial gradient from affine drift and bounds the gradient bias under smooth nonlinear drift in terms of the temporal span of the sample set, which is kept at $O(n)$ evaluations by first-in-first-out sample replacement. Rather than forming an explicit second-order step Hessian from $2n+1$ noisy, drifting evaluations, the algorithm retains Powell's fourth-order kernel in the KKT system as a spatial covariance weight for unmodeled quadratic residuals, and pairs the resulting generalized least-squares gradient and drift estimators with a zero-Hessian adaptive cubic regularization step, a drift-compensated acceptance test, and a covariance-based stopping rule. Together, these components achieve an $O(\varepsilon^{-2})$ worst-case evaluation complexity above the statistical noise floor.

On the Mor\'e--Wild benchmark collection, TOBYQA performs comparably to Py-BOBYQA on stationary noisy problems and achieves substantially higher solve rates than existing derivative-free solvers when the observation channel drifts over time. Our ablation experiments confirm that drift compensation in the acceptance test, the fourth-order curvature kernel in the KKT system, and adaptive cubic regularization are all required to prevent premature stagnation, whereas propagating incremental Hessian updates across iterations or computing trial steps from an explicit interpolated Hessian at $m = 2n+1$ exposes the solver to noise amplification and nonlinear drift distortion. Future work includes incorporating higher-order or localized temporal basis functions to track rapidly oscillating channels without degrading the conditioning of the KKT system, extending the method to bound and linear constraints, and generalizing the framework from drifting observation channels to time-varying objectives $f(\vct{x}, t)$ whose minimizers move over time.
