\subsection{Soft-Constrained Quadratic Model}
\label{sec:kkt-derivation}

We build on the underdetermined interpolation framework introduced by Powell in NEWUOA~\cite{Powell2006NEWUOA}. At each iteration, NEWUOA maintains $m$ interpolation points around the current iterate and constructs a quadratic surrogate $Q \approx f$. Because a full quadratic model in $\mathbb{R}^n$ has $(n+1)(n+2)/2$ coefficients, determining all of them by exact interpolation requires $O(n^2)$ evaluations. Powell instead proposed using $m = 2n+1$ points~\cite{Powell2004LeastFrobenius}: the interpolation conditions $Q(\vct{x}_i) = F_i$ determine the constant and gradient while providing partial curvature information, and the remaining freedom in the Hessian is resolved by solving
\[
  \min_{Q}\; \|\nabla^2 Q - \nabla^2 Q_{\mathrm{prev}}\|_F
  \quad \text{subject to} \quad Q(\vct{x}_i) = F_i, \quad i = 1, \dots, m.
\]
This construction presupposes a stationary, noiseless objective. Under the dynamic observation channel~\eqref{eq:oracle_intro}, sequential sampling interleaves spatial displacement with temporal evolution, so a static polynomial model projects temporal shifts onto spatial directions and biases the gradient. Moreover, enforcing $Q(\vct{x}_i) = F_i$ exactly propagates observation noise directly into the fitted coefficients.

TOBYQA addresses both issues by augmenting the quadratic model with a linear temporal term and replacing exact interpolation with a regularized least-squares fit. At iteration $k$, let $\vct{x}_k$ be the current center and $t_k$ its evaluation timestamp. Given the active set $\mathcal{Y}_k = \{(\vct{x}_i, t_i, F_i)\}_{i=1}^m$ of $m = 2n+1$ samples, we center the spatial displacements $\vct{d}_i := \vct{x}_i - \vct{x}_k$ and temporal offsets $\theta_i := t_i - t_k$ at $(\vct{x}_k, t_k)$, and consider the time-augmented model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q-model}
  Q_k(\vct{x},t) \;=\; c + \vct{g}^{\!\top}(\vct{x} - \vct{x}_k) + \tfrac{1}{2}(\vct{x} - \vct{x}_k)^{\!\top}G\,(\vct{x} - \vct{x}_k) + \gamma\,(t - t_k),
\end{equation}
where $c = Q_k(\vct{x}_k, t_k) \in \mathbb{R}$, $\vct{g} = \nabla_{\vct{x}} Q_k(\vct{x}_k, t_k) \in \mathbb{R}^n$, and $\gamma = \partial_t Q_k(\vct{x}_k, t_k) \in \mathbb{R}$ represent the local function value, spatial gradient, and instantaneous drift rate at $(\vct{x}_k, t_k)$, and $G = G^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ is the symmetric model Hessian. To accommodate observation noise, we fit these parameters by solving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:soft-obj}
  \min_{c,\,\vct{g},\,G=G^\top,\,\gamma}\;
  \tfrac{1}{4}\|G\|_F^2
  \;+\;
  \tfrac{\rho}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}
  \bigl(Q_k(\vct{x}_i, t_i) - F_i\bigr)^2,
\end{equation}
where $\rho>0$ balances curvature regularization against data fitting. Unlike NEWUOA, which penalizes the incremental change $\|\nabla^2 Q - \nabla^2 Q_{\mathrm{prev}}\|_F$ to accumulate second-order information across iterations, problem~\eqref{eq:soft-obj} penalizes $\tfrac{1}{4}\|G\|_F^2$ directly. In a time-varying channel, the previous Hessian $G_{\mathrm{prev}} = \nabla^2 Q_{k-1}$ was fitted around an earlier timestamp $t_{k-1}$ and absorbs unmodeled nonlinear drift from past iterations; penalizing $\|G - G_{\mathrm{prev}}\|_F^2$ would lock these historical drift errors into the KKT right-hand side even after the old samples themselves are discarded from $\mathcal{Y}_k$. Penalizing $\tfrac{1}{4}\|G\|_F^2$ resets the curvature prior at each iteration and prevents cross-iteration drift accumulation (see the ablation study in Section~\ref{sec:ablations}, Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).

Solving~\eqref{eq:soft-obj} directly in the primal variables would require optimizing over the $n(n+1)/2$ independent entries of $G$. To reduce the dimension of the linear system from $O(n^2)$ to $O(n)$, we pass to a dual representation of $G$ by introducing the fitting residuals $\xi_i := F_i - Q_k(\vct{x}_i, t_i)$ and rewriting~\eqref{eq:soft-obj} as the constrained problem
\begin{equation}\label{eq:soft-constrained}
  \min_{c,\,\vct{g},\,G=G^\top,\,\gamma,\,\vct{\xi}}\;
  \tfrac{1}{4}\|G\|_F^2 + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\sum_{i=1}^m \xi_i^2
  \quad \text{s.t.} \quad Q_k(\vct{x}_i, t_i) + \xi_i = F_i, \;\; i = 1, \dots, m,
\end{equation}
with Lagrangian
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lagrangian}
  \mathcal{L}(c, \vct{g}, G, \gamma, \vct{\xi}, \vct{\lambda})
  \;=\;
  \tfrac{1}{4}\|G\|_F^2 + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\sum_{i=1}^m \xi_i^2
  - \sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i \Bigl(Q_k(\vct{x}_i, t_i) + \xi_i - F_i\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Setting the partial derivatives of $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to the primal variables to zero gives the first-order optimality conditions:
\begin{align}
  \nabla_G \mathcal{L} = 0 &\implies G \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i\,\vct{d}_i\,\vct{d}_i^{\!\top}, \label{eq:stat-G} \\
  \nabla_c \mathcal{L} = 0 &\implies \sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i \;=\; 0, \label{eq:stat-c} \\
  \nabla_{\vct{g}} \mathcal{L} = 0 &\implies \sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i \vct{d}_i \;=\; \vct{0}, \label{eq:stat-g} \\
  \nabla_\gamma \mathcal{L} = 0 &\implies \sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i \theta_i \;=\; 0, \label{eq:stat-gamma} \\
  \nabla_{\xi_i} \mathcal{L} = 0 &\implies \xi_i \;=\; \tfrac{1}{\rho}\lambda_i. \label{eq:stat-xi}
\end{align}
Identity~\eqref{eq:stat-G} expresses the $n\times n$ matrix $G$ in terms of the $m$ dual multipliers $\vct{\lambda} = (\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_m)^\top$. Substituting~\eqref{eq:stat-G} and $\xi_i = \lambda_i/\rho$ into $Q_k(\vct{x}_i, t_i) + \xi_i = F_i$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-i}
  \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^m \lambda_j (\vct{d}_i^\top \vct{d}_j)^2 + c + \vct{d}_i^\top \vct{g} + \gamma \theta_i + \tfrac{1}{\rho}\lambda_i \;=\; F_i,
  \qquad i = 1, \dots, m.
\end{equation}
Combining~\eqref{eq:row-i} with the orthogonality conditions~\eqref{eq:stat-c}--\eqref{eq:stat-gamma} produces the $(m+n+2)\times(m+n+2)$ saddle-point system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tobyqa-kkt}
  \begin{bmatrix}
     A + \tfrac{1}{\rho}I_m & \mathbf{1}_m & D & \vct{\theta} \\
    \mathbf{1}_m^{\!\top} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
    D^{\!\top} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
    \vct{\theta}^{\!\top} & 0 & 0 & 0
  \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix}\vct{\lambda}\\c\\\vct{g}\\\gamma\end{bmatrix}
  \;=\;
  \begin{bmatrix}\vct{F}\\0\\\vct{0}\\0\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times m}$ has entries $A_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\vct{d}_i^\top \vct{d}_j)^2$, $D \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ has rows $\vct{d}_i^\top$, $\vct{\theta} = (\theta_1, \dots, \theta_m)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^m$, and $\vct{F} = (F_1, \dots, F_m)^\top$. We denote the augmented constraint block by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Xaug-def}
  X_{\mathrm{aug}} \;:=\;
  \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{1}_m & D & \vct{\theta}\end{bmatrix}
  \;\in\;\mathbb{R}^{m\times(n+2)}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Solvability of the KKT System}
\label{sec:well-posedness}

To compute $(c, \vct{g}, \gamma)$ from~\eqref{eq:tobyqa-kkt} at each iteration, we now establish when the linear system is uniquely solvable and derive an explicit expression for the primal parameters. Writing $K := A + \rho^{-1} I_m$, the coefficient matrix of~\eqref{eq:tobyqa-kkt} takes the block form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-block}
  M \;=\;
  \begin{bmatrix}
    K & X_{\mathrm{aug}} \\
    X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top & 0
  \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For a saddle-point matrix of the form~\eqref{eq:M-block} with symmetric positive definite $K$, block Gaussian elimination shows that $M$ is nonsingular if and only if the Schur complement $S := X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top K^{-1} X_{\mathrm{aug}}$ is nonsingular, which holds if and only if $X_{\mathrm{aug}}$ has full column rank. We therefore impose the following rank condition on the active set.

\begin{definition}
\label{def:rank}
The active set $\mathcal{Y}_k = \{(\vct{x}_i, t_i, F_i)\}_{i=1}^m$ satisfies the augmented column-rank condition if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-rank}
  \operatorname{rank}(X_{\mathrm{aug}}) \;=\; n + 2,
\end{equation}
that is, the constant vector $\mathbf{1}_m$, the spatial displacement columns of $D$, and the temporal offset vector $\vct{\theta}$ are linearly independent.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:solvability}
Let $K = A + \rho^{-1} I_m \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times m}$, where $A_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\vct{d}_i^\top \vct{d}_j)^2$ and $\rho > 0$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[\rm(i)]
  \item $K$ is symmetric and strictly positive definite ($K \succ 0$).
  \item If $\mathcal{Y}_k$ satisfies Definition~\ref{def:rank}, the Schur complement
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:schur-S}
    S \;:=\; X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top K^{-1} X_{\mathrm{aug}} \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{(n+2)\times(n+2)}
  \end{equation}
  is symmetric and strictly positive definite ($S \succ 0$).
  \item The matrix $M$ in~\eqref{eq:M-block} is nonsingular, and the unique primal solution is given by
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:primal-sol}
    \begin{bmatrix}c\\\vct{g}\\\gamma\end{bmatrix}
    \;=\;
    S^{-1} X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top K^{-1} \vct{F}
    \;=:\;
    \Pi \vct{F},
  \end{equation}
  where $\Pi := S^{-1} X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top K^{-1} \in \mathbb{R}^{(n+2)\times m}$ is the linear recovery operator. The dual variable is $\vct{\lambda} = K^{-1}(\vct{F} - X_{\mathrm{aug}} \Pi \vct{F})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For part (i), let $\vct{v} \in \mathbb{R}^{m}$ be nonzero. Since each entry of $A$ satisfies
\[
  A_{ij} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}(\vct{d}_i^\top \vct{d}_j)^2 \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}\bigl((\vct{d}_i \vct{d}_i^\top)(\vct{d}_j \vct{d}_j^\top)\bigr),
\]
$A$ is the Gram matrix of the rank-one matrices $\{\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \vct{d}_i \vct{d}_i^\top\}_{i=1}^m$ under the Frobenius inner product, so $A \succeq 0$. For any $\rho > 0$, $\vct{v}^\top K \vct{v} = \vct{v}^\top A \vct{v} + \rho^{-1}\|\vct{v}\|_2^2 \ge \rho^{-1}\|\vct{v}\|_2^2 > 0$, which proves $K \succ 0$.

For part (ii), let $\vct{u} \in \mathbb{R}^{n+2}$ be nonzero. By Definition~\ref{def:rank}, $X_{\mathrm{aug}}$ has full column rank, so $\vct{w} := X_{\mathrm{aug}} \vct{u} \ne \vct{0}$. Because $K \succ 0$, its inverse $K^{-1} \succ 0$. Hence $\vct{u}^\top S \vct{u} = \vct{w}^\top K^{-1} \vct{w} > 0$, which proves that $S \succ 0$.

For part (iii), the saddle-point matrix $M$ has invertible upper-left block $K$ and invertible Schur complement $-S$. Setting $\vct{p} := [c, \vct{g}^\top, \gamma]^\top$, standard block elimination yields $\vct{\lambda} = K^{-1}(\vct{F} - X_{\mathrm{aug}}\vct{p})$. Substituting this into $X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top \vct{\lambda} = \vct{0}$ gives $S \vct{p} = X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top K^{-1} \vct{F}$, which yields~\eqref{eq:primal-sol}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:poisedness}
In unregularized minimum-Frobenius-norm interpolation~\cite{ConnScheinbergVicente2009,Powell2006NEWUOA}, the upper-left block $A$ is only positive semidefinite, so nonsingularity of the KKT matrix requires $A$ to be positive definite on the null space of $X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top$ (quadratic poisedness). Here, the ridge term $\rho^{-1} I_m$ ensures $K \succ 0$ on all of $\mathbb{R}^m$, reducing solvability to the first-order rank condition~\eqref{eq:full-rank}.
\end{remark}

Geometrically, condition~\eqref{eq:full-rank} requires that the spatial displacements span $\mathbb{R}^n$ and that the temporal offset vector $\vct{\theta}$ not lie in $\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{1}_m, D\}$, so that elapsed time is not an affine function of spatial position across the sample set. The next result verifies that the initial coordinate stencil used by TOBYQA automatically satisfies this requirement under sequential evaluation.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:axis-rank}
Let $\mathcal{Y}_0 = \{(\vct{x}_i, t_i, F_i)\}_{i=1}^{2n+1}$ be the initial active set formed by evaluating the coordinate stencil $\{\vct{x}_0\} \cup \{\vct{x}_0 \pm \Delta_0 \vct{e}_j\}_{j=1}^n$ at strictly increasing timestamps $t_1 < t_2 < \dots < t_{2n+1}$, with $\vct{x}_1 = \vct{x}_0$ evaluated at $t_1$. Then $\operatorname{rank}(X_{\mathrm{aug}}) = n + 2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the displacement matrix $D \in \mathbb{R}^{(2n+1)\times n}$, the rows for $\vct{x}_0 \pm \Delta_0 \vct{e}_j$ are $\pm\Delta_0 \vct{e}_j^\top$, so the columns of $D$ are linearly independent and $\operatorname{rank}(D) = n$. Moreover, the row of $D$ at the center sample is zero while the corresponding entry of $\mathbf{1}_{2n+1}$ is one, so $\mathbf{1}_{2n+1}$ is independent of the columns of $D$. Hence $\operatorname{rank}([\mathbf{1}_{2n+1} \;\; D]) = n+1$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $\vct{\theta} \in \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{1}_{2n+1}, D\}$, so that $\theta_i = \alpha + \vct{\beta}^\top \vct{d}_i$ for some $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ and $\vct{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^n$. The center row gives $\alpha = \theta_1 = 0$. Applying this to the $j$-th probe pair, whose displacements are $\pm\Delta_0 \vct{e}_j$ and whose offsets are $\theta_{2j}$ and $\theta_{2j+1}$, gives
\[
  \theta_{2j} = \alpha + \Delta_0 \beta_j,
  \qquad
  \theta_{2j+1} = \alpha - \Delta_0 \beta_j
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \alpha = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\theta_{2j} + \theta_{2j+1}\bigr).
\]
Because probe timestamps strictly succeed the center timestamp, $\tfrac{1}{2}(\theta_{2j}+\theta_{2j+1}) > 0$, contradicting $\alpha = 0$. Therefore $\vct{\theta} \notin \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{1}_{2n+1}, D\}$, and $\operatorname{rank}(X_{\mathrm{aug}}) = n+2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Gradient Estimation under Drift}
\label{sec:drift-decoupling}

Having obtained the explicit estimator $[c, \vct{g}^\top, \gamma]^\top = \Pi \vct{F}$ in~\eqref{eq:primal-sol}, we now examine how temporal drift in the observations $\vct{F}$ affects the recovered spatial gradient $\vct{g}$ and drift rate $\gamma$. From~\eqref{eq:schur-S} and~\eqref{eq:primal-sol}, the recovery operator $\Pi = S^{-1} X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top K^{-1}$ is a left inverse of the augmented constraint matrix:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:left-inverse}
  \Pi X_{\mathrm{aug}} \;=\; S^{-1} \bigl(X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top K^{-1} X_{\mathrm{aug}}\bigr) \;=\; I_{n+2}.
\end{equation}
Because $\mathbf{1}_m$ is the first column of $X_{\mathrm{aug}}$ and $\vct{\theta}$ is the $(n+2)$-nd column, identity~\eqref{eq:left-inverse} implies $\Pi \mathbf{1}_m = \vct{e}_1$ and $\Pi \vct{\theta} = \vct{e}_{n+2}$. Any constant or linear-in-time component in $\vct{F}$ is therefore mapped entirely into the $c$- and $\gamma$-coordinates, with zero contribution to the gradient coordinates $\vct{g}$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:exact-decoupling}
Suppose the observation channel exhibits affine drift:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-channel}
  F(\vct{x}, t) \;=\; f(\vct{x}) + \beta\,t + \varepsilon(\vct{x},t),
\end{equation}
for some unknown drift rate $\beta \in \mathbb{R}$. Let $\vct{F}^{\mathrm{stat}} := \vct{F} - \beta \vct{t} \in \mathbb{R}^m$ denote the static observations that would be obtained in the absence of drift, and let $[c^{\mathrm{stat}}, (\vct{g}^{\mathrm{stat}})^\top, \gamma^{\mathrm{stat}}]^\top := \Pi \vct{F}^{\mathrm{stat}}$ be the model parameters recovered from $\vct{F}^{\mathrm{stat}}$. If $\operatorname{rank}(X_{\mathrm{aug}}) = n+2$, then the parameters recovered by TOBYQA from the drifting observations $\vct{F}$ satisfy:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decoupling-identity}
  \vct{g} \;=\; \vct{g}^{\mathrm{stat}},
  \qquad
  \gamma \;=\; \gamma^{\mathrm{stat}} + \beta,
  \qquad
  c \;=\; c^{\mathrm{stat}} + \beta\,t_k.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\vct{t} = (t_1, \dots, t_m)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^m$ be the vector of query timestamps. Because $t_i = t_k + \theta_i$, we can write $\vct{t}$ in terms of the columns of $X_{\mathrm{aug}} = [\mathbf{1}_m \;\; D \;\; \vct{\theta}]$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t-decomp}
  \vct{t} \;=\; t_k\,\mathbf{1}_m + \vct{\theta} \;=\; X_{\mathrm{aug}}\,(t_k \vct{e}_1 + \vct{e}_{n+2}),
\end{equation}
where $\vct{e}_1 = (1, 0, \dots, 0)^\top$ and $\vct{e}_{n+2} = (0, \dots, 0, 1)^\top$ in $\mathbb{R}^{n+2}$.
Applying $\Pi$ to $\vct{F} = \vct{F}^{\mathrm{stat}} + \beta \vct{t}$ and using~\eqref{eq:left-inverse} yields
\begin{align*}
  \begin{bmatrix}c\\\vct{g}\\\gamma\end{bmatrix}
  \;=\; \Pi \vct{F}
  &\;=\; \Pi \vct{F}^{\mathrm{stat}} + \beta\,\Pi X_{\mathrm{aug}}\,(t_k \vct{e}_1 + \vct{e}_{n+2}) \\
  &\;=\; \begin{bmatrix}c^{\mathrm{stat}}\\\vct{g}^{\mathrm{stat}}\\\gamma^{\mathrm{stat}}\end{bmatrix}
  + \beta\,(t_k \vct{e}_1 + \vct{e}_{n+2})
  \;=\; \begin{bmatrix}c^{\mathrm{stat}} + \beta t_k\\\vct{g}^{\mathrm{stat}}\\\gamma^{\mathrm{stat}} + \beta\end{bmatrix},
\end{align*}
which gives~\eqref{eq:decoupling-identity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:significance}
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-decoupling} is an instance of the Frisch--Waugh--Lovell theorem~\cite{FrischWaugh1933,Lovell1963} in weighted least squares: including the temporal offset $\vct{\theta}$ as an explicit regressor projects out linear temporal trends for any drift rate $\beta$, any noise realization, and any sample radius.
\end{remark}

When the drift $\mu(t)$ is nonlinear, its Taylor expansion around $t_k$ decomposes into an affine component $\mu(t_k) + \mu'(t_k)(t - t_k)$ plus a second-order remainder. By~\eqref{eq:left-inverse}, the recovery operator $\Pi$ still annihilates the affine component, so the estimation bias in $\vct{g}$ and $\gamma$ is determined solely by the second-order temporal remainder across the active set.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:nonlinear-drift}
Suppose the observation channel has the form $F(\vct{x},t) = f(\vct{x}) + \mu(t) + \varepsilon(\vct{x},t)$, where $\mu \in C^2(\mathbb{R})$ with $|\mu''(t)| \le L_\mu$ for all $t$. Let $\tau_{\max} := \max_{i} |t_i - t_k|$ denote the temporal span of the active set $\mathcal{Y}_k$. Then:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlinear-bounds}
  \bigl|\gamma - \gamma^{\mathrm{stat}} - \mu'(t_k)\bigr| \;\le\; C_\gamma\,L_\mu\,\tau_{\max},
  \qquad
  \bigl\|\vct{g} - \vct{g}^{\mathrm{stat}}\bigr\|_2 \;\le\; C_g\,L_\mu\,\tau_{\max}^2,
\end{equation}
where $C_\gamma, C_g > 0$ depend on the normalized active set configuration and $\rho$, but are independent of $\tau_{\max}$ and $L_\mu$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Taylor expanding $\mu(t_i)$ around $t_k$ gives $\mu(t_i) = \mu(t_k) + \mu'(t_k)\theta_i + r_\mu(t_i)$, where $|r_\mu(t_i)| \le \tfrac{1}{2} L_\mu \theta_i^2 \le \tfrac{1}{2} L_\mu \tau_{\max}^2$. In vector form,
\[
  \vct{\mu} \;=\; X_{\mathrm{aug}}\bigl[\mu(t_k)\, \vct{e}_1 + \mu'(t_k)\, \vct{e}_{n+2}\bigr] + \vct{r}_\mu.
\]
By~\eqref{eq:left-inverse}, $\Pi$ annihilates the first term, so the perturbation from $\vct{\mu}$ is governed solely by $\vct{r}_\mu$:
\[
  \begin{bmatrix}c - c^{\mathrm{stat}} - \mu(t_k)\\\vct{g} - \vct{g}^{\mathrm{stat}}\\\gamma - \gamma^{\mathrm{stat}} - \mu'(t_k)\end{bmatrix}
  \;=\; \Pi\,\vct{r}_\mu.
\]
Writing $\tau := \tau_{\max}$ and factoring the temporal column as $\vct{\theta} = \tau\,\vct{\hat\theta}$ with $\|\vct{\hat\theta}\|_\infty \le 1$, we set $T := [\mathbf{1}_m \;\; D \;\; \vct{\hat\theta}]$ and $J := \operatorname{diag}(I_{n+1}, \tau)$, so that $X_{\mathrm{aug}} = TJ$. Then $S = J\,\hat S\,J$ with $\hat S := T^\top K^{-1} T$. Because $K$ depends only on the spatial displacements and $\rho$, both $K$ and $\hat S$ are independent of $\tau$, and
\[
  \Pi\, \vct{r}_\mu \;=\; J^{-1} \hat S^{-1} T^\top K^{-1} \vct{r}_\mu,
\]
where $J^{-1} = \operatorname{diag}(I_{n+1}, \tau^{-1})$. The $(c,\vct{g})$-components carry the factor $1$, while the $\gamma$-component carries $\tau^{-1}$. Combining this with $\|\vct{r}_\mu\|_2 \le \tfrac{\sqrt{m}}{2} L_\mu \tau^2$ yields~\eqref{eq:nonlinear-bounds} with $C_\gamma = C_g = \tfrac{\sqrt{m}}{2}\,\|\hat S^{-1} T^\top K^{-1}\|_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:fifo-bound}
Theorem~\ref{thm:nonlinear-drift} shows that the temporal span $\tau_{\max}$ directly controls the gradient error from nonlinear drift. The sample replacement rule in Section~\ref{sec:sample-management} removes the oldest non-center sample at each iteration to keep $\tau_{\max}$ bounded.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Residual Covariance Model}
\label{sec:statistical-foundation}

In the optimization algorithm of Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}, TOBYQA uses only the first-order parameters $(c, \vct{g}, \gamma)$ from~\eqref{eq:primal-sol} to generate trial steps, setting the explicit model Hessian in the step subproblem to zero ($H_k \equiv 0$) because estimating $n(n+1)/2$ curvature entries from $m = 2n+1$ noisy, drifting evaluations amplifies errors of order $O(\sigma_\varepsilon / \delta_k^2)$. A natural question is why we retain the fourth-order kernel $A_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\vct{d}_i^\top \vct{d}_j)^2$ inside $K = A + \rho^{-1} I_m$ when estimating $(c, \vct{g}, \gamma)$, and how the regularization weight $\rho$ should be calibrated to the observation noise variance $\sigma_\varepsilon^2$.

To answer both questions, consider a twice continuously differentiable objective $f$ and its Taylor expansion around $\vct{x}_k$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-decomp}
  f(\vct{x}_k + \vct{d}_i) \;=\; f(\vct{x}_k) + \nabla f(\vct{x}_k)^\top \vct{d}_i + \tfrac{1}{2} \vct{d}_i^\top H \vct{d}_i + r_3(\vct{d}_i),
\end{equation}
where $H := \nabla^2 f(\vct{x}_k)$ and $|r_3(\vct{d}_i)| = O(\|\vct{d}_i\|_2^3)$. When the observations $F_i = f(\vct{x}_i) + \mu(t_i) + \varepsilon_i$ are fitted by the linear terms $c + \vct{g}^\top \vct{d}_i + \gamma \theta_i$, the unmodeled spatial residual at sample $i$ is dominated by the quadratic form $q_i := \tfrac{1}{2}\vct{d}_i^\top H \vct{d}_i$. Unlike the independent observation noise $\varepsilon_i$, the curvature residuals $q_i$ and $q_j$ at two displacements $\vct{d}_i$ and $\vct{d}_j$ are spatially correlated through the shared Hessian $H$. The following result shows that under an isotropic prior on $H$, their covariance matrix is proportional to $A$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:kernel-stat}
Let $H \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ be a random symmetric matrix drawn from a rotation-invariant ensemble with $\mathbb{E}[H] = 0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:goe-prior}
  \mathbb{E}[H_{ab} H_{cd}] \;=\; \sigma_H^2\,(\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd} + \delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_H^2 > 0$ represents the curvature variance scale. Then for any displacement vectors $\vct{d}_i, \vct{d}_j \in \mathbb{R}^n$, the covariance between $q_i := \tfrac{1}{2} \vct{d}_i^\top H \vct{d}_i$ and $q_j := \tfrac{1}{2} \vct{d}_j^\top H \vct{d}_j$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cov-kernel}
  \operatorname{Cov}(q_i, q_j) \;=\; \tfrac{\sigma_H^2}{2}\,(\vct{d}_i^\top \vct{d}_j)^2 \;=\; \sigma_H^2 A_{ij}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Writing $q_i = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{a,b=1}^n H_{ab} d_{i,a} d_{i,b}$, we have $\mathbb{E}[q_i] = 0$, and
\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{Cov}(q_i, q_j)
  &\;=\; \mathbb{E}[q_i q_j]
  \;=\; \tfrac{1}{4} \sum_{a,b,c,d=1}^n \mathbb{E}[H_{ab} H_{cd}]\,d_{i,a} d_{i,b} d_{j,c} d_{j,d} \\
  &\;=\; \tfrac{\sigma_H^2}{4} \sum_{a,b,c,d=1}^n (\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd} + \delta_{ad}\delta_{bc})\,d_{i,a} d_{i,b} d_{j,c} d_{j,d} \\
  &\;=\; \tfrac{\sigma_H^2}{4} \Bigl((\vct{d}_i^\top \vct{d}_j)^2 + (\vct{d}_i^\top \vct{d}_j)^2\Bigr)
  \;=\; \tfrac{\sigma_H^2}{2} (\vct{d}_i^\top \vct{d}_j)^2
  \;=\; \sigma_H^2 A_{ij}.
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:kernel-stat}, the sum of the curvature residual $\vct{q} = (q_1, \dots, q_m)^\top$ and the independent observation noise $\vct{\varepsilon} = (\varepsilon_1, \dots, \varepsilon_m)^\top$ has composite covariance
\[
  \Sigma_{\mathrm{res}} \;=\; \sigma_H^2 A + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 I_m \;=\; \sigma_H^2 \Bigl(A + \frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{\sigma_H^2} I_m\Bigr) \;=\; \sigma_H^2 \bigl(A + \rho^{-1} I_m\bigr) \;=\; \sigma_H^2 K,
\]
when the regularization weight is set to the curvature-to-noise ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:snr-rho}
  \rho \;=\; \frac{\sigma_H^2}{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}.
\end{equation}
Under this covariance model, the generalized least-squares estimator of the linear parameters $[c, \vct{g}^\top, \gamma]^\top$ is
\[
  \bigl(X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top \Sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^{-1} X_{\mathrm{aug}}\bigr)^{-1} X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top \Sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^{-1} \vct{F}
  \;=\; \bigl(X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top K^{-1} X_{\mathrm{aug}}\bigr)^{-1} X_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top K^{-1} \vct{F}
  \;=\; \Pi \vct{F},
\]
which coincides with the primal KKT solution~\eqref{eq:primal-sol}. Retaining the kernel $A$ in $K = A + \rho^{-1}I_m$ therefore accounts for the spatial covariance of unmodeled quadratic curvature when estimating $(c, \vct{g}, \gamma)$, while relation~\eqref{eq:snr-rho} provides the calibration rule for $\rho$ used in Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}.
